# A larger cone for the type B Eulerian transformation

## Theorem

Let \(\mathcal B(t^m)=B_m(x)\), where the \(B_m\) are defined by the recurrence in the supplied problem. For each \(n\ge1\), let

\[
\mathcal C_n=\left\{\sum_{k=0}^n\alpha_k(t-1)^{n-k}(t+1)^k:
\alpha_k\ge0\right\}.
\]

If \(p\in\mathcal C_n\) is nonzero, then \(\mathcal B(p)\) has exactly \(n\) distinct nonpositive real roots. The root zero occurs exactly when \(p(1)=0\), equivalently \(\alpha_n=0\). When \(n\ge2\), the ordinary Eulerian polynomial \(A_n(x)\) strictly interlaces \(\mathcal B(p)(x)\).

This proves both targets in the prompt. In fact, \(\mathcal C_n\) is the cone generated by the monic degree-\(n\) polynomials whose roots belong to \([-1,1]\). Indeed,

\[
t+a=\frac{1-a}{2}(t-1)+\frac{1+a}{2}(t+1)\qquad(-1\le a\le1),
\]

so any product of these linear factors belongs to \(\mathcal C_n\); the endpoint generators themselves are such products.

## 1. The refined ordinary Eulerian polynomials

Define

\[
p_{n,j}(x)=(1-x)^{n+1}\sum_{m\ge0}m^j(m+1)^{n-j}x^m,
\qquad 0\le j\le n.
\tag{1}
\]

All series identities below are formal rational-function identities. In particular, they are not evaluated as convergent series at negative real numbers outside the unit disk.

These polynomials satisfy

\[
p_{0,0}=1,\qquad
p_{n,j}=x\sum_{i<j}p_{n-1,i}+\sum_{i\ge j}p_{n-1,i}.
\tag{2}
\]

For completeness, the two finite sums of the summands from (1) telescope:

\[
\sum_{i<j}m^i(m+1)^{n-1-i}=(m+1)^n-m^j(m+1)^{n-j},
\]

\[
\sum_{i\ge j}m^i(m+1)^{n-1-i}=m^j(m+1)^{n-j}-m^n.
\]

After summing over \(m\), the extra terms cancel because
\(x\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^n x^m=\sum_{m\ge0}m^n x^m\) for \(n\ge1\). This proves (2), and in particular proves polynomiality.

The standard interlacing-preservation lemma for (2) gives the interlacing sequence

\[
(p_{n,0},p_{n,1},\ldots,p_{n,n}).
\tag{3}
\]

A precise primary source is Athanasiadis, *On the real-rootedness of the Eulerian transformation*, arXiv:2302.00754v5, Lemma 2.2(b) and Example 2.3; Equation (4) there is (2). This is the only substantive external mathematical dependency in this proof.

In particular,

\[
p_{n,0}=A_n,\qquad p_{n,n}=xA_n,\qquad A_n\preceq p_{n,j}.
\tag{4}
\]

The ordinary Eulerian recurrence proves that \(A_n\) has \(n-1\) simple negative roots and that \(A_{n-1}\) strictly interlaces \(A_n\). At any root \(r\) of \(A_n\), (4) therefore gives

\[
\frac{p_{n,j}(r)}{A_n'(r)}\le0.
\tag{5}
\]

The term \(j=1\) is strictly negative: expanding \(m=(m+1)-1\) gives

\[
p_{n,1}=A_n-(1-x)A_{n-1},\qquad
\frac{p_{n,1}(r)}{A_n'(r)}
=(r-1)\frac{A_{n-1}(r)}{A_n'(r)}<0.
\tag{6}
\]

The last inequality uses \(A_{n-1}(r)/A_n'(r)>0\), which follows directly from strict interlacing and positive leading coefficients.

## 2. A positive expansion at every negative test point

The defining type B recurrence gives

\[
\frac{B_m(x)}{(1-x)^{m+1}}=\sum_{\ell\ge0}(2\ell+1)^m x^\ell.
\tag{7}
\]

The case \(m=0\) is the geometric series, and applying \(2x\,d/dx+1\) verifies the induction step. Consequently, for any polynomial \(p\),

\[
\mathcal B(p)(x)=(1-x)\sum_{\ell\ge0}
p\big((1-x)(2\ell+1)\big)x^\ell.
\tag{8}
\]

First take \(p(t)=\prod_{i=1}^n(t+a_i)\), where every \(a_i\in[-1,1]\). The key identity is

\[
(1-x)(2\ell+1)+a_i
=(1+a_i-x)(\ell+1)+(1-a_i-x)\ell.
\tag{9}
\]

Define \(W_j(x)\) by

\[
\sum_{j=0}^n W_j(x)y^j
=\prod_{i=1}^n\big((1+a_i-x)+(1-a_i-x)y\big).
\tag{10}
\]

Substituting (9) into (8), expanding (10), and using (1) proves the polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{(1-x)^n\mathcal B(p)(x)=\sum_{j=0}^n W_j(x)p_{n,j}(x).}
\tag{11}
\]

For every \(r<0\), both coefficients in every linear factor of (10) are strictly positive, including when \(a_i=\pm1\). Thus

\[
W_j(r)>0\quad(0\le j\le n).
\tag{12}
\]

At a root \(r\) of \(A_n\), equations (5), (6), (11), and (12) imply

\[
\frac{\mathcal B(p)(r)}{A_n'(r)}<0.
\tag{13}
\]

The same strict inequality holds for every nonzero nonnegative combination of these products, hence for every nonzero \(p\in\mathcal C_n\).

For the endpoint basis \(h_{n,k}=\mathcal B((t-1)^{n-k}(t+1)^k)\), the weights are explicitly

\[
\sum_jW_{n,k,j}(x)y^j
=(-x+(2-x)y)^{n-k}(2-x-xy)^k.
\tag{14}
\]

## 3. Exact root count

Let \(F=\mathcal B(p)\) for a nonzero \(p\in\mathcal C_n\). Each endpoint generator is monic of degree \(n\), and \(B_n\) is monic of degree \(n\), so \(F\) has degree \(n\) and positive leading coefficient. Since \(B_m(0)=1\),

\[
F(0)=p(1)=2^n\alpha_n\ge0.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(n\ge2\), write the roots of \(A_n\) as
\(r_1<\cdots<r_{n-1}<0\). Equation (13) gives

\[
\operatorname{sgn}F(r_i)=(-1)^{n-i}.
\]

The signs at \(-\infty,r_1,\ldots,r_{n-1}\) alternate. Hence there is a root in each of the first \(n-1\) intervals. If \(F(0)>0\), there is one further root in \((r_{n-1},0)\). If \(F(0)=0\), zero itself is the further root. In both cases there are \(n\) distinct roots, so these exhaust the degree, and every root is simple. The roots of \(A_n\) lie strictly between consecutive roots of \(F\).

For \(n=1\),
\(F=\alpha_0x+\alpha_1(x+2)\), which has one nonpositive root and has root zero exactly when \(\alpha_1=0\). For \(n=0\), outputs are nonnegative constants. The zero input is handled by the conventional inclusion of the zero polynomial among real-rooted polynomials.

## 4. Consequences for the two requested transformations

For the main map, \(t^{n-k}(1+t)^k\in\mathcal C_n\) because
\(t=((t-1)+(t+1))/2\). Moreover, its value at \(t=1\) is \(2^k>0\). Every nonzero nonnegative combination in the question therefore transforms to a polynomial with \(n\) distinct strictly negative roots.

For the secondary map, binomial expansion gives

\[
\mathcal D^B(p(t))=\mathcal B(p(t-1)).
\tag{16}
\]

Thus

\[
d_{n,k}:=\mathcal D^B(t^{n-k}(1+t)^k)
=\mathcal B((t-1)^{n-k}t^k),
\tag{17}
\]

whose input also lies in \(\mathcal C_n\). Any nonzero nonnegative combination \(\sum_k c_kd_{n,k}\) has \(n\) distinct nonpositive roots. Its constant term is \(c_n\), so zero is a root exactly when \(c_n=0\); otherwise every root is strictly negative.

The reduced positive-weight identity for this secondary basis is

\[
(1-x)^{n-k}d_{n,k}(x)
=\sum_j[y^j]\big((-x+(2-x)y)^{n-k}(1+y)^k\big)p_{n,j}(x).
\tag{18}
\]

This common-interlacer proof alone does not establish ordered pairwise interlacing within either row. An independent operator proof establishes that stronger assertion; the secondary extension is in `DB_OPERATOR_APPENDIX.md`.

## 6. Prior art and scope

The secondary conjecture was already answered by Lily Li Liu and Xue Yan, *Some interlacing properties related to the Eulerian and derangement polynomials*, Advances in Applied Mathematics 162 (January 2025), article 102776:

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2024.102776
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0196885824001088

Its abstract identifies the type B and colored derangement transformations, and a later paper by the same authors describes the full cone statement and row-interlacing result:

- Xue Yan and Lily Li Liu, *Some interlacing sequences related to the Eulerian and derangement polynomials*, Discrete Mathematics 348(11) (November 2025), article 114598.
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2025.114598
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X25002067

The papers' full text could not be retrieved in this bounded literature check. The proof in Sections 1–4 is independent of their uninspected arguments. The packet's description of the secondary target as unresolved is therefore out of date. No novelty claim is made for the larger cone theorem.
